The measurement that rebuilt the product

Everyone building on a taste graph starts by assuming they know who the audience is — a city, a country, a genre. We tested that against the API before building on it. Pin a catalogue and vary filter.location.query across New York, São Paulo, Tokyo, Mumbai and Berlin: the affinities come back identical to four decimal places. Location does not move film taste in Qloo's model at all. It is honoured for places; for film it does nothing.

So the agent stops assuming who an audience is and describes it by what it already plays and watches.

A frontier model is a strong baseline and we are not claiming otherwise. Its set of five is steady across repeats; its ordering is not reliably reproducible, so we compare sets and never quote a place from it. Rank correlation sits about 1.01 standard errors from zero: read it as no agreement, not as measured opposition.

What the model cannot do is decline. It ranked every title confidently where this run withheld one, and it cannot report which titles rank the same for every audience.

It is allowed to say no

A shipped preset that refuses outright.

Springsteen, Beyoncé and Taylor Swift are three audiences wearing one city's name. Their two readings agree at 0.13, so anything measured against them inherits that instability. The agent says so and stops. A tool that always answers is not one you can licence against.

Honest limits

  • The two readings of an audience are disjoint, not independent: they share no entities, but both come out of the same graph.
  • Every pair that qualified in the sweep fell below the separation floor. The selection holds; the order within it is less certain.
  • 15 of the 18 map audiences are too small to split into halves that can disagree, and are drawn dashed rather than implying a check that did not run.
  • Divergence from a language model is not evidence the model is wrong. There is no ground truth for what a programmer would licence.
  • Qloo knows brands, not products: films, artists, places, books, shows, podcasts and games — not arbitrary retail SKUs.
  • Every number here is a snapshot of the graph in October 2026.

The business case

Programmers at niche SVOD and FAST channels, distributors and international sales agents all make the same decision repeatedly — which titles for which audience — with genre tags and a model's sense of fame as the substitutes for taste data. The input is a list they already keep.

We have not sold this, so there is no pricing number here. The shape we would test first is per-seat for a programming team with a catalogue tier above it, priced against one wrong licensing decision rather than per query — because the product sometimes answers "not enough evidence", and that answer should not cost more.
